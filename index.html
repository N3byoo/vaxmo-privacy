Vaxmo — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

In short: your missions, settings and photos are stored on your phone. Three things leave it: what you give AI capture (a typed plan, the words of a voice plan, or a photo of a written plan), which is sent to our server and to Google's Gemini AI to be turned into missions; once a day, when you close your day, the titles of tomorrow's missions, your tools and a few measured facts, so Gemini can word your morning push (you can turn this off in Settings); and crash reports, which contain no missions, words or photos. Vaxmo also keeps a record of how you use the app. That record itself stays on your phone. There are no accounts and no advertising.

What stays on your phone

Vaxmo saves the following in your phone's private app storage. Unless a section below says otherwise, none of it is sent anywhere:

  • Your missions — titles, notes, priorities, dates and times, and whether they're done
  • Your routines (missions that repeat) and their history
  • Your tools (counters, streaks and countdowns), the words each one came from, and what they have counted
  • Tomorrow's morning push and tool order, once they are decided
  • Photos you attach to a mission — resized copies in the app's private storage. The originals in your camera or gallery are never changed.
  • Today's capture log — what you captured today and the photos you captured, so you can review them. It clears itself the next day.
  • Your settings — reminder times and display preferences
  • Your answers to the short setup questions shown when you first open the app
  • Scheduled reminder and alarm identifiers, so they can be updated or cancelled
  • A record of how you use Vaxmo — for example when a mission was created (and whether by typing, voice, photo or by hand), completed, moved or deleted; when you used AI capture; which screen was open and for how long; whether you opened a reminder. It contains no mission titles, notes, words or photos, only the app's internal reference numbers. Vaxmo keeps the full record for 90 days, and after that only daily totals. It is never sent anywhere: not to our server, not to Google and not in crash reports.
  • Facts worked out from that record, such as which days you finish the most. You can read and reset them under Profile, in What Vaxmo knows about you. They are worked out on your phone. The only time any leave it is for the morning push, described below.

Android keeps this storage private to Vaxmo: other apps can't read it. Uninstalling Vaxmo permanently deletes all of it.

AI capture: what leaves your phone

AI capture is optional. Nothing is sent unless you use it, and you can always add missions yourself instead. Each time you send a capture, Vaxmo sends:

  • What you captured: the plan you typed; or the words of a voice plan (as text — never the recording); or the photo you took or picked, resized and re-saved as a new JPEG, which leaves out the original photo's hidden details such as its location; or, when Vaxmo asks you a follow-up question, that question, the part of your plan it was about, and your answer.
  • Your phone's local date, time and time zone, and your morning and evening reminder times, so "tomorrow" and "tonight" land on the right day and time.
  • A random install number created by the app, used only to count your AI captures for the day (there is a daily limit). It isn't linked to your name, account, phone number or any device identifier.

It is sent, encrypted (HTTPS), to two places:

1. Our server (Vaxmo API, run on Cloudflare)

  • It passes your capture to Gemini and sends the suggested missions back to your phone. It does not store your capture or the result.
  • It counts how many captures you made today, to apply the daily limit. The count is stored under a code made from your install number that changes every day and can't be turned back into it. Counts are deleted at the daily reset (midnight in California, US).
  • Its logs record counts, timings and error types — for example "a photo capture, 3 missions, 1.4 seconds". They never contain your words, photos or results, or your install number. Logs are deleted after 3 days.
  • Like any website, Cloudflare, which runs the server, needs your IP address to deliver each request. Our server doesn't record your IP address or location: we have turned off the request logs that would keep them.

2. Google (the Gemini API)

  • Google's Gemini AI reads your capture and suggests missions. Google receives the capture and the context listed above. It does not receive your install number, your IP address or anything that identifies you.
  • Google keeps what it receives for up to 55 days to detect abuse, as its Gemini API usage policy describes.
  • Vaxmo currently uses Gemini's free service. Under the Gemini API terms, Google may use what is sent to the free service to improve its products and AI models, and people at Google may review it, after Google separates it from our account. Please don't put sensitive or confidential information (such as health details, passwords or ID numbers) in a capture.

Vaxmo never sells or shares your captures with anyone else, and nothing from a capture is used for advertising.

The morning push: what leaves your phone

When you finish your evening review, Vaxmo makes one request to word tomorrow's morning push and to choose which of your tools come first. That is at most once a day. You can turn it off in Settings under AI morning push; Vaxmo then words the morning on your phone, and none of this is sent. If tomorrow has no missions, nothing is sent either. Each request contains:

  • Facts Vaxmo has measured, once there is enough data behind them, in plain sentences such as "You finish the most on Tuesdays (84%)", and how many days or missions each is based on. A fact you have reset isn't sent.
  • Tomorrow's missions: each title, whether it is critical, and its time. Never its notes or photos.
  • Your tools: each name, kind and numbers (for example a count, a goal, a streak or days left).
  • The date, your local time and time zone, and the random install number described above. The install number is used only to allow one request a day, and it doesn't reach Google.

It is never your notes, your captures, your photos, your setup answers, or the record of how you use Vaxmo. Our server and Google treat this request exactly as they treat a capture, as described in sections 1 and 2 above: our server doesn't store it, and on the free service Google may keep it for up to 55 days, use it to improve its products, and have people review it. Code on our server and on your phone checks the answer, and a push containing a number that wasn't in the request is never shown. The push itself is scheduled on your phone, so it arrives without any further connection.

Voice capture

When you hold or tap the mic, Android's speech recognition service on your phone turns your speech into text. That service is part of Android (on most phones it is provided by Google), and it handles your voice under its own privacy terms. Vaxmo receives only the resulting text, never a recording, and stores no audio.

Crash reports

If Vaxmo crashes, it sends a crash report to Sentry, a crash-reporting service (Functional Software, Inc.), so we can find and fix the bug. A report contains:

  • the error and where in Vaxmo's code it happened
  • your phone's model and Android version, and the Vaxmo version

It never contains your missions, routines, captures, voice words or photos, your install number, or any account or contact details. Vaxmo does not send usage analytics, session tracking or performance data. Like any service on the internet, Sentry receives your IP address when a report arrives. We have set it not to store that address. Crash reports are deleted after 90 days at most.

Notifications and alarms

Reminders and alarms are scheduled by Android on your phone. Vaxmo does not use push notifications, so no reminder content goes through any outside service.

Permissions, and why they are needed

  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS — to show your reminders.
  • SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM — to ring reminders and alarms at the time you set. If you decline, reminders still work but Android may deliver them a few minutes late.
  • USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT — so an alarm can open over the lock screen.
  • RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED — to restore your reminders and alarms after your phone restarts.
  • RECORD_AUDIO (microphone) — only while you hold or tap the mic for voice capture.
  • CAMERA — only when you take a photo, to attach it to a mission or to capture a written plan.
  • INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — to send AI captures, the morning push request and crash reports, and to know when you're offline so a capture can wait until you're back online.

Choosing a photo from your gallery uses Android's system photo picker, which shares only the single photo you pick. Vaxmo does not request access to your photo library.

The home screen widget

The optional widget reads your missions from the same private storage on your phone in order to show them. It sends nothing.

Age

Vaxmo is meant for adults (18 and over), and it isn't directed at children. The Gemini API that powers AI capture and the morning push may only be used by people aged 18 or over.

Deleting your data

  • On your phone: use Clear All inside the app, or uninstall Vaxmo. Either removes everything permanently, including photos, tools, the capture log and the record of how you use the app. Deleting a mission or a photo also deletes its photo file.
  • On our server: nothing from your captures or morning push is stored there. The daily capture count is deleted at the daily reset, and logs after 3 days.
  • At Google: captures and morning push requests are kept for up to 55 days, as described above. They are separated from our account, and we have no way to find or delete yours.
  • Crash reports: deleted after 90 days at most. If you want reports from your phone deleted sooner, contact us with the date and time of the crash.
